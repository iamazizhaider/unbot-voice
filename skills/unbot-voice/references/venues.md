# Venue guides

Humanizing removes tells. It doesn't make everything casual. Each venue has its own common tells and its own legitimate conventions that you should keep.

## LinkedIn and social posts
- **Common tells:** the "I almost didn't post this" hook; "Here's the thing:", "The result?", "And honestly?"; one-line paragraphs stacked for drama; "No X. No Y. Just Z."; emoji bullets; a lesson or moral at the end; "journey" and "growth" metaphors; hashtag walls.
- **Keep:** first person, short paragraphs (a few, not all), a concrete number if there is one.
- **Aim for:** one story with real details, told in the order it happened. End when the story ends. A plain observation beats a moral.

## Email (cold, internal, status updates)
- **Common tells:** "I hope this email finds you well", "I wanted to reach out", "truly impressed", "companies like yours", "support your journey", bold-label bullet summaries, "Let me know if you have any questions!", sign-offs with exclamation marks.
- **Keep:** a subject line that states the point, a greeting, and a clear ask with a time or date.
- **Aim for:** lead with the reason for writing. Write one specific problem and one specific proof, not three benefits. For status updates, give dates, causes and owners. Cold emails should be under 120 words.

## Blog posts and articles
- **Common tells:** stock openers ("In today's fast-paced..."), "In this article, we'll delve into...", "From X to Y" titles, "Whether you're a beginner or an expert", H2s in Title Case, "Key Takeaways" boxes, a "Conclusion" heading that recaps everything.
- **Keep:** headings if the post is long and scannable, examples, and the writer's opinions.
- **Aim for:** open with a claim, a problem or a scene, not context-setting. Cut the recap at the end. Use sentence-case headings.

## Academic and student writing
- **Common tells:** "It is important to note", "plays a crucial role", "a complex interplay", "On one hand... on the other hand", stacked "Furthermore / Moreover / Additionally", "In conclusion", weasel attributions ("studies show").
- **Keep:** formal register, hedging that reflects real uncertainty ("the data suggest"), citations, and discipline-specific terms.
- **Aim for:** a thesis in the first paragraph. Name every source you lean on. Transitions should show logic ("because", "so", "but"), not just signal "next point".
- **Policy:** if the user is submitting to a course or journal with an AI policy, improve the writing but mention the policy. Don't present humanizing as a way to avoid disclosure.

## Encyclopedic and Wikipedia-style
- **Common tells:** promotional words (nestled, renowned, vibrant, rich history), "serves as", "is a testament", "Awards and Recognition" sections, "Challenges and Legacy" or "Future Outlook" sections, "-ing" analysis tails, "has been featured in" lists, Markdown syntax in wikitext, title-case headings.
- **Keep:** neutral tone, "is" and "was", dates and figures, attribution in text ("According to the county tourism office...").
- **Aim for:** facts in plain declarative sentences. Uniform sentence rhythm is normal here, so leave it alone.
- **Policy:** Wikipedia restricts LLM-generated content. If the text is headed for Wikipedia, point that out.

## Technical docs, READMEs, specs
- **Common tells:** "This powerful tool seamlessly...", "robust", "leverage", "empowers developers", emoji section headers, a "Conclusion" section, marketing tone in the opening line.
- **Keep:** structure, headings, bullet lists of steps, code blocks, tables. Structure is correct here.
- **Aim for:** first line says what it is and what it does. Use the imperative for instructions ("Run X"). Skip adjectives about your own software.

## Product and marketing copy
- **Common tells:** "Introducing the X — the Y that redefines Z", "meticulous attention to detail", "seamlessly blends", "elevates your experience to new heights", "It's not just an X — it's a statement", "Your mornings will never be the same."
- **Keep:** benefit-first framing, energy, short sentences.
- **Aim for:** one concrete spec and why it matters to the buyer ("holds temperature within 1°C, so light roasts don't taste sour"). Claims you can check beat claims you can't.

## Fiction and personal essays
- **Common tells:** "a testament to", "the weight of unspoken words", eyes that "sparkle with mischief", rule-of-three descriptions, every paragraph ending on an epiphany, characters explaining their feelings in full sentences, "little did they know".
- **Keep:** the writer's style quirks, even ones you'd personally edit out.
- **Aim for:** concrete sensory detail over abstract emotion. Leave some things unsaid. Allow scenes to end without a lesson.
