# AI writing pattern catalog

**Last updated: November 2026.** Tells shift with each model generation. See "Keeping this current" at the bottom.

Sources: Wikipedia "Signs of AI writing" (WP:AISIGNS) and Hunting the Muse, "How to tell if writing is AI". These signs are descriptive, not proof. Density and co-occurrence matter more than any single hit. Humans use many of these too, since LLMs learned them from human editorials, blogs and marketing copy.

---

## 1. Content patterns

### Regression to the mean
Specific facts get replaced with generic, flattering significance.
- Before: "inventor of the first train-coupling device"
- After (AI): "a revolutionary titan of industry"
- **Fix:** restore the concrete fact. If there isn't one, cut the claim.

### Inflated significance, legacy and trends
Watch for: *stands as / serves as*, *is a testament / reminder*, *a crucial / pivotal / vital / significant / key role / moment*, *underscores its importance*, *setting the stage for*, *key turning point*, *indelible mark*, *has shaped conversations / discussions / debate about*.
- **Fix:** say what happened. Let readers judge significance.

### Notability and media-coverage padding
Watch for: *independent coverage*, *regional media outlets*, *cited / featured / profiled in*. The text lists where something was covered instead of what was said.
- **Fix:** report the substance, or drop the line.

### Over-reliance on directories and profiles
Watch for: *is listed in*, *identifies / describes ... as*, *professional profile lists*, *maintains an active social media presence*.

### Superficial "-ing" analysis
A trailing participle clause adds fake insight: *highlighting*, *underscoring*, *emphasizing*, *ensuring*, *reflecting*, *symbolizing*, *contributing to*, *fostering*, *enhancing*, *valuable insights*, *aligns / resonates with*.
- Example: "The festival draws 10,000 visitors, highlighting the town's vibrant cultural scene."
- **Fix:** "The festival draws 10,000 visitors." Stop there.

### Vague connection
Watch for: *in connection with*, *connected to*, *associated with*, *particularly / widely associated*. The writer is hiding that they don't know the actual relationship.

### Weasel attributions
Watch for: *Industry reports*, *Observers have cited*, *Experts argue*, *Some critics argue*, *several sources* (when one is cited), and *such as* in front of a vague list.
- **Fix:** name the source or cut the claim.

### Formulaic challenges and legacy endings
The pattern runs "Despite its X, [subject] faces challenges...", then "Despite these challenges...", often under headings like "Challenges and Legacy", "Future Outlook" or "Awards and Recognition".
- **Fix:** delete the paragraph unless the challenges are specific and sourced.

### Hedging disclaimers and corrective framing
- 2023–24: *it's important to note*, *it's worth noting*, unsolicited safety or legal caveats.
- 2024–25: *not publicly available*, *maintains a low profile*, used to justify speculation.
- 2026: *X rather than Y*, *does not by itself establish*, *should not be confused with*, *while specific details are limited*, *based on available information*.

## 2. Language and grammar

### AI vocabulary by era
One word is fine. A cluster is the tell.
- **2023–mid-2024:** additionally, boasts, bolstered, crucial, delve, emphasizing, enduring, garner, intricate, interplay, landscape, meticulously, pivotal, underscore, tapestry, testament, valuable, vibrant
- **Mid-2024–mid-2025:** align with, enhance, fostering, highlighting, showcasing
- **Mid-2025–mid-2026:** emphasizing, enhance, highlighting, showcasing
- **Mid-2026 onward:** challenge, clearer, dependable, echoed, foster, leverage, matters, multifaceted, practical, prioritize, quietly, steady, universally (plus a uniform 8–20-word sentence cadence with lots of periods and commas)
- **Grok-flavored:** causal, empirical, correlate, underscore
- **Marketing and LinkedIn:** unlock, empower, elevate, navigate (and journey or navigation metaphors), grounded, game-changer, seamless, robust, cutting-edge, transformative, holistic, synergy
- **Use only when literally needed:** crucial, significant, important, critical, leverage, foster

### Promotional or travel-brochure tone (mostly 2023–25)
*boasts a*, *vibrant*, *rich (heritage / history)*, *profound*, *showcasing*, *nestled*, *in the heart of*, *groundbreaking*, *renowned*, *diverse array*, *breathtaking*, *must-visit*.

### Copula avoidance
AI replaces "is / are / has" with *serves as*, *stands as*, *marks*, *functions as*, *represents*, *boasts*, *features*, *maintains*, *offers*, *refers to*. Use of "is/are" in academic writing dropped more than 10% after 2023.
- **Fix:** write "is". Write "has".

### Negative parallelism
- *Not only X but also Y* / *It's not just X, it's Y*
- *It's not X, it's Y* / *Not X, but Y*
- *No X. No Y. Just Z.*
- *Y rather than X* (common in 2026)
- **Fix:** state Y.

### Rule of three
"adjective, adjective, adjective" or "short phrase, short phrase, and short phrase", used to make thin analysis look complete.
- **Fix:** keep only the items that matter.

### Elegant variation
Cycling synonyms for the same thing ("the regime", "the Soviet government", "the authorities") to avoid repetition.
- **Fix:** pick one name and reuse it.

### Stiff synonyms for plain words
*authored* (wrote), *passed away* (died), *utilize* (use), *commence* (start), *facilitate* (help), *individuals* (people), *in order to* is fine, and humans use it a lot.

## 3. Forced voice and stock framing (Hunting the Muse)

### Manufactured drama and sass
*But here's the thing:*, *Here's the deal*, *Here's why that matters*, *Then I realized:*, *The result?*, *Hot take:*, *And honestly?*, *Let that sink in.*, *That's the real unlock.*, *Read that again.*
- **Fix:** usually just "But", or nothing.

### Stock openers and clichés
*In today's fast-paced digital landscape*, *In the dynamic world of...*, *As the world continues to evolve...*, *In an era where...*, *Shouting into the void*, *No fluff*, *looming challenges*, *understanding the advantages and disadvantages*, *double-edged sword*, *to new heights*, *will never be the same*, *redefines the way*, *unlock your full potential*, *more than just a*, *has never been more crucial*, *the key lies in*, *warrants careful consideration*, *unprecedented opportunities*.

### Email boilerplate
*I hope this email finds you well*, *I wanted to reach out*, *I wanted to provide a quick update*, *was truly impressed by*, *forward-thinking companies like yours*, *support your journey*.

### Audience-straddling and both-sides framing
*Whether you're a seasoned pro or just starting out...*, *On one hand... on the other hand...*, which hedges in place of a position.

### Formulaic titles
*From X to Y*, *Master X in N Days*, *The Ultimate Guide to X*, *Unlocking the Power of X*, *X: A Deep Dive*.

## 4. Style and formatting

- **Em dashes:** used heavily for asides where a comma, parenthesis or colon fits better, sometimes spaced " — ". Overcorrecting is a newer tell too: some tuned models now avoid all varied punctuation.
- **Bold overuse** inside prose.
- **Inline-header lists:** "**Label:** explanation" bullets everywhere.
- **Bullets where prose belongs**, and sudden lists mid-argument.
- **Title Case Headings** where the venue uses sentence case.
- **Emoji** at the start of headings or bullets (🚀 ✅ 💡).
- **Curly quotes** in plain-text venues.
- **Markdown leaking** into non-markdown places: `**`, `#`, `---`, `[text](url)`, code fences.
- **Summary closers:** *In summary*, *In conclusion*, *Overall*, *Ultimately*, and a recap of every paragraph's point.
- **"X and Y" section headings** ("History and Development", "Challenges and Legacy").

## 5. Chatbot residue

- Pleasantries: *Certainly!*, *Great question!*, *I hope this helps*, *Absolutely*, *Happy to help*
- Offers: *Would you like me to...*, *Let me know if...*, *Here is a...*, *Feel free to...*
- Self-reference: *As a large language model*, *As an AI*, *As of my last knowledge update*, *up to my last training update*
- Instructions to the user left in the text: *Delete this section before submitting*
- Placeholders: *[Your Name]*, *[Company]*, *2025-XX-XX*, *INSERT_SOURCE_URL*, *example.com*
- Tool artifacts: *contentReference[oaicite:0]*, *utm_source=chatgpt.com*, stray *↩*
- Abrupt cut-offs mid-sentence

## 6. What human writing tends to have

- Simple *is / has / there is* constructions
- Plain verbs: *wrote*, *died*, *used*, *said*
- Committed superlatives: *one of the best*, *was the first*
- Selective qualifiers and intensifiers: *very*, *perhaps*, *tends to*, *kind of*
- Some wordiness and imperfection: *in order to*, *the fact that*
- Consistent naming, with no synonym rotation
- Uneven rhythm, digressions, and opinions the writer can defend
- Specifics only the writer would know: names, numbers, dates, what actually happened
- The ability to explain why each choice was made

## 7. Not reliable on their own

Perfect grammar, mixed registers, bland or fancy prose, transition words alone (*additionally*, *consequently*, *notably*), unsourced claims. AI detectors such as GPTZero and Pangram have non-trivial error rates, and humans detect AI at close to chance (about 57–64% in one study). Never present an audit as proof.

## Keeping this current

The vocabulary lists are dated by model era because they change: "delve" was the 2023 tell, while "quietly" and "steady" are 2026 tells. Em dashes faded as a signal once models were tuned against them, and that tuning produced its own tell (flat, uniform punctuation). To refresh:

1. Re-read the [Wikipedia guide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing), especially its "AI vocabulary" table and any new section dated to the current year.
2. Add new words to the right era in section 2 and to the matching regex in `scripts/patterns.json`. The CLI, the GitHub Action and the browser playground all read that file.
3. Move anything the guide now lists as historical into a "historical" note rather than deleting it, since older text still carries it.
4. Run `python3 -m unittest discover -s tests` from the repo root. It checks that AI drafts still score high, rewrites and human prose score low, and quoted mentions are ignored.
5. Bump the "Last updated" date above.
